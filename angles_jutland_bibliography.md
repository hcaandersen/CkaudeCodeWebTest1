# Bibliography: The Angles on the Jutland Peninsula

## Primary Ancient Sources

**Tacitus, Publius Cornelius.** *Germania (De origine et situ Germanorum)*. c. 98 AD.
- The earliest surviving written mention of the Angles (Anglii) and their worship of the goddess Nerthus
- Chapter 40 describes the Angles as one of the remote Suebic tribes
- Available at: https://facultystaff.richmond.edu/~wstevens/history331texts/barbarians.html

**Ptolemy, Claudius.** *Geography (Geographia)*. c. 150 AD.
- Ancient geographical work mentioning the Angles (Sueboi Angeilloi)
- Originally written in Greek at Alexandria
- Available at: https://penelope.uchicago.edu/Thayer/E/Gazetteer/Periods/Roman/_Texts/Ptolemy/home.html

**Bede, the Venerable.** *Historia ecclesiastica gentis Anglorum (Ecclesiastical History of the English People)*. c. 731 AD.
- Book 2 provides crucial information about the Angles' homeland in Angulus
- States that Angeln "remained desert to this day" after the migration
- Full text available at: https://www.gutenberg.org/files/38326/38326-h/38326-h.html
- Also: https://www.ccel.org/ccel/bede/history.v.i.xiv.html

**Anonymous.** *Widsith*. Date uncertain, preserved in 10th century Exeter Book.
- Old English heroic poem containing legendary kings of the Angles

**Anonymous.** *Beowulf*. Date uncertain, preserved in 11th century manuscript.
- Old English epic poem containing references to Angle kings and legends

**Anonymous.** *Anglo-Saxon Chronicle*. Various dates, compiled from 9th century onwards.
- Contains genealogies of Angle kings including legendary pre-migration rulers

## Notes on Sources

**Reliability**: This bibliography includes a mix of primary ancient sources (Tacitus, Ptolemy, Bede), modern academic sources, peer-reviewed archaeological research, museum resources, and reputable encyclopedias. Where possible, multiple sources were consulted to verify facts.

**Dating**: Many events in Angle history, particularly from the legendary period and early migration, lack precise dates. Dates given in the timeline represent scholarly consensus where available, with approximate dates indicated by "c." (circa).

**Archaeological Evidence**: The physical evidence from sites like Thorsberg moor, Nydam, and various cremation cemeteries provides concrete data that complements and sometimes corrects the ancient textual sources.

**Web Sources**: All web sources were accessed in October 2025. Due to the nature of web content, some URLs may change over time. Major reference works like Wikipedia articles have been cited with their stable article titles for future reference.
